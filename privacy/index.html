artvibe — Privacy Policy

Effective: November 27, 2026

artvibe ("the app") shows you the story behind the music you're listening to: artist and album background, credits, reviews, and upcoming shows. It is developed by Aleksei Verein, who is the data controller for the purposes of the GDPR (contact: tpom6oh+artvibe@gmail.com).

This policy explains what data the app touches, what actually leaves your device, and what we keep. It also covers the artvibe website and the artvibe web app.

What the app reads on your device

To know what you're listening to, artvibe uses Android's Notification Access (Notification Listener) permission to read the media metadata of the currently playing track — the artist name, track title, album name, artwork, and playback state exposed by your music player. This works with any player (Spotify, YouTube Music, etc.).

The app reads only media-playback metadata. It does not read, store, or transmit the content of your other notifications (messages, emails, or anything else). Non-music playback (podcasts, videos) is filtered out and ignored.

Your listening history and cached track information are stored locally on your device and can be removed by clearing the app's storage or uninstalling the app.

What leaves your device

1. Track lookups. To build the card, the app queries public music-data services directly from your device. They receive different things:

ServiceWhat it receives
MusicBrainzartist name, track title, album name
Wikipediathe artist, album, song or record label being looked up
Cover Art Archivea MusicBrainz identifier for the album — no names
Event Art Archivea MusicBrainz identifier for a show or festival you open — no names
ListenBrainz (similar artists)a MusicBrainz identifier for the artist — no names
CritiqueBrainza MusicBrainz identifier for the album — no names
Ticketmasterthe artist name only — no track or album

As with any request your phone makes, each one also carries your IP address, which those services see. We send no account, no device identifier, and nothing that links one lookup to another. The requests are ephemeral: we operate no server, so they are never collected, stored, or built into a history anywhere but on your own device. Each service is an independent controller and handles what it receives under its own privacy policy. These lookups are what the app is for, so they cannot be switched off separately; not granting Notification Access stops them entirely.

2. Anonymous usage analytics and crash reports — only if you agree. artvibe asks on first launch, with the switch off. If you leave it off, no analytics software is started, no identifier is created, and nothing in this section happens. If you turn it on, the app sends anonymous events to PostHog (hosted in the European Union), which we use to understand how the app is used and to fix what is broken. This includes:

  • an anonymized identifier: a one-way cryptographic hash (SHA-256) of your device's Android ID. Your actual Android ID never leaves the device, and the hash cannot be reversed into it;
  • usage events: which screens are viewed, whether track identification succeeded and how long it took, and taps on features (for example, expanding a story, following a credit, or opening a ticket link). These events do not include the names of tracks or artists you listen to;
  • crash reports: when the app crashes, the type of error and the line of our code it happened in. Error messages are deliberately excluded, because they can quote the track being processed;
  • basic device context: device model, OS version, app version, locale, timezone, and screen size;
  • approximate location: the country, region and city that your IP address resolves to. This comes from the network address the request arrives on — the app never asks for location access and never reads your device's location. The coordinates stored with it are the centre of that city, not where you are.

3. Wrong-match reports — only when you tap "Not right?". Identifying a track from a title is imprecise, and the only way to fix a bad match is to see it. When you tap "Not right?", the app sends the artist, title and album your player reported, together with the match we got wrong and how confident we were.

This report is sent unlinkably. It carries no device identifier, no session, and none of the device context (model, OS, locale) attached to the events above — a freshly generated random id is used once and immediately discarded, so we cannot tell which reports came from the same person. It is a fact about a track, not a record about you. Nothing is sent unless analytics is switched on, and nothing is sent unless you tap the button.

Two limits, stated plainly rather than glossed over. First, the report goes out the moment you tap, so it lands close in time to your other activity; we hold no identifier connecting the two, but timing by itself carries some information. Second, suppressing the IP address stops the report being stored against your address — it cannot stop our analytics provider's servers seeing the connection that delivers it, as any server does. The report is unlinkable in the data we hold, not unobservable.

You can turn this off at any time in Settings → Privacy & data. Turning it off stops all collection immediately and discards anything not yet sent.

4. Your ListenBrainz account — only if you connect one. ListenBrainz is an open listening-history service run by the non-profit MetaBrainz Foundation. Under Settings → ListenBrainz you can paste your ListenBrainz user token; the app checks it with ListenBrainz and stores it on your device, encrypted with a key that never leaves it. Connecting sends nothing by itself — each use below has its own switch, off until you turn it on:

  • Send my listens: each track you play past half its length (or four minutes) goes to your ListenBrainz account — the artist, title and album, when it started, how long you listened, which player app played it, and the MusicBrainz identifiers the app matched it to. While a track plays, it is also shown as "playing now" on your profile. ListenBrainz listening histories are public. Listens made offline wait on your device and are sent later. Podcasts and other non-music playback are never sent.
  • Ask ListenBrainz first when matching: the artist, title and album of each track are sent to ListenBrainz's matcher before MusicBrainz is searched.
  • Love: tapping "Love on ListenBrainz" on a card marks that recording loved on your profile.

These go from your device straight to ListenBrainz, under your account; they never pass through us, and we keep no copy. ListenBrainz is an independent controller of your account's data under its own privacy policy. Disconnecting deletes the token from your device and anything still waiting to be sent; listens already sent stay on ListenBrainz, where you can delete them.

We do not collect your name, email address, contacts, precise location, or any account information. The app has no accounts of its own; connecting a ListenBrainz account is optional and stays between your device and ListenBrainz.

All network traffic uses encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS).

The artvibe website

The artvibe website (artvibe.web.app) counts visits so we can tell which pages and which links bring people to the app. It does this without cookies: nothing is stored on or read from your device, which is why there is no cookie banner.

  • What is recorded: that a page was viewed and which version of it (the main page, /samples, /sleeve or /thread); the ?ref= tag of the link you followed, if it had one; whether you are on Android, an iPhone or a computer; how far down the page you scrolled; and whether you tapped the link to Google Play.
  • How a visit is counted without cookies: PostHog (in the European Union) combines your IP address and browser user agent with a secret value that changes every day, and turns them into a one-way hash. Your IP address is then discarded, not stored. The hash lets us count one visit as one visit on the same day; it cannot be turned back into your IP address, and the next day you count as a new visitor.
  • What is not recorded: no cookies or local storage, no profile of you, no session recordings, no location (not even the country), no tracking across other websites, no advertising.

The site is hosted on Firebase Hosting (Google), which, like any web server, sees your IP address when it delivers the page. The link to Google Play carries a tag naming the page version and the ?ref= source, so Play's statistics can tell which page a store visit came from; it carries nothing about you.

The artvibe web app

The web app at artvibe.uk lets you search MusicBrainz and open the same pages as the app, in a browser.

  • Lookups go straight from your browser: to the same public services the app uses (MusicBrainz, Wikipedia and Wikidata, Cover Art Archive, Event Art Archive, ListenBrainz, CritiqueBrainz). They receive what you search for or the identifier of the page you open, and, as with any website, your IP address. On the Now playing page, the username you type is sent to ListenBrainz or Last.fm, whichever you chose, to read what that account is playing right now; both services show this publicly.
  • Kept in your browser only if you agree, never sent to us: answers from MusicBrainz, for up to seven days, so pages you have already seen open faster (browser storage, IndexedDB); the last Now playing username that worked, so the link goes straight back to it; and whether you closed the note about the Android app. On your first visit the site asks whether it may keep the first two; saying no changes nothing else, the answers then stay in memory only until you close the tab and the username is not remembered. Your answer is kept so the question isn't repeated, and you can change it at any time with the Browser storage link at the bottom of every page; turning it off deletes what was kept. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes everything.
  • Counted without cookies, the same way as the website above: which kind of screen was shown (search, song, artist, album, label and so on, never which one); how many songs and artists a search found, not what you searched for; that the Now playing page was started and with which service, not the username, and whether the playing track was matched; and taps on the links to Google Play and MusicBrainz. Page addresses are cut down to the kind of page before anything is sent, so no search, username or page identifier reaches the analytics. No profile of you, no session recordings, no location (not even the country or time zone), no tracking across other websites, no advertising.
  • Link previews: when a link to a page is shared, the site looks up that page's title and cover at MusicBrainz, Wikidata and the Cover or Event Art Archive so the preview can show them, and keeps the result for a week. This concerns the page, not who shared or opened it.

The web app is hosted on Cloudflare, which, like any web server, sees your IP address when it delivers the page.

Your rights

If you are in the EU or UK you have the right to access, correct, erase, restrict, or object to the processing of your personal data, the right to data portability, and the right to withdraw consent at any time. You may also lodge a complaint with your national data protection authority.

Because the only identifier we hold is an anonymous hash, we cannot find your records from your name or email. Settings → Privacy & data shows the identifier for your device — include it in your request and we can act on it. Email tpom6oh+artvibe@gmail.com; we respond within 30 days.

Where analytics data is processed

Analytics data, from the app, the website and the web app, is stored and processed by PostHog on servers in the European Union, under a data processing agreement. PostHog acts only as our processor and not for its own purposes. Nothing else reaches us: the only code we run online is the web app's link-preview lookup on Cloudflare, which looks up the page being shared and keeps no record of who asked. The public music-data services listed above receive only a track query and are independent controllers under their own policies.

Data retention and deletion

Analytics data is stored by PostHog on our behalf. We do not set an automatic expiry on it, so assume it is kept for as long as the project exists.

You can have everything collected from your device erased at any time: email tpom6oh+artvibe@gmail.com with the identifier shown in Settings → Privacy & data, and we will delete those records within 30 days. The full steps, and exactly what is and is not deleted, are on the data deletion page.

Visits to the website and the web app cannot be looked up or deleted per person either: once the IP address is discarded, nothing in them points to a visitor beyond that day.

The one thing we cannot delete on request is a wrong-match report, because it carries nothing identifying the device that sent it — being unable to find your reports is precisely what makes them anonymous. They contain a track name and no trace of you.

Local data (history, caches, your ListenBrainz token and any listens waiting to be sent) never reaches our servers and is deleted when you clear the app's storage or uninstall it.
